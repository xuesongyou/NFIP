    Methodology

    This dashboard shows the NFIP residential takeup rate, i.e., the share of residential structures with an active National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) contract, for every census block group in the 50 states and the District of Columbia. It reports the takeup rate for all residential structures, as well as those inside and outside the Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA), respectively. The SFHA is the FEMA-mapped floodplain with a 1% chance of flooding in any given year.

    How NFIP residential takeup rates are calculated:

    NFIP residential takeup rate = active NFIP residential contracts ÷ residential structures
    NFIP residential takeup rate in SFHA = active NFIP residential contracts in the SFHA ÷ residential structures in the SFHA
    NFIP residential takeup rate outside SFHA = active NFIP residential contracts outside the SFHA ÷ residential structures outside the SFHA

    Residential structure counts come from the USACE National Structure Inventory (NSI) 2022. Each structure is assigned to a census block group using Census Bureau boundary files, and to inside or outside the SFHA using the FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL). Active NFIP residential contracts come from the OpenFEMA NFIP Redacted Policies - v3 dataset.

    County, state, and national rates on the dashboard are calculated by summing counts across block groups and then dividing, not by averaging block group rates. This weights each area by its number of residential structures.

    Data sources:

    • NFIP residential contract-in-force: OpenFEMA dataset NFIP Redacted Policies - v3. Contracts are counted as active as of the date in the asOfDate field (currently —). A contract is considered residential if the occupancy type of the insured structure is coded as one of the following: 1 (Single family residence), 2 (2 to 4 Unit residential building), 3 (Residential building with more than 4 units), 11 (Single-family residential building with the exception of a mobile home or a single residential unit within a multi unit building), 12 (A residential non-condo building with 2, 3, or 4 units seeking insurance on all units), 13 (A residential non-condo building with 5 or more units seeking insurance on all units), 14 (Residential mobile/manufactured home), 15 (Residential condo association seeking coverage on a building with one or more units), or 16 (Single residential unit within a multi-unit building).
    • Residential structures: USACE National Structure Inventory (NSI) 2022 dataset. Residential only.
    • Flood zones: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL). Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs) are labeled as Zone A, Zone AO, Zone AH, Zones A1-A30, Zone AE, Zone A99, Zone AR, Zone AR/AE, Zone AR/AO, Zone AR/A1-A30, Zone AR/A, Zone V, Zone VE, and Zones V1-V30.
    • Census block group boundaries: 2020 cartographic boundary files from the U.S. Census Bureau.

    Important notes:

    • Data for residential properties only.
    • Private flood insurance is not included in these estimates.
    • NFIP residential contracts that could not be assigned to a census block group are included in state and national takeup rates (and in county rates where the county is known), but do not appear on the block group map or in the block group table.
    • The data is a snapshot of NFIP contracts in force on one date. Takeup changes as contracts lapse and renew.
    • A NFIP contract may cover several units, but a single residential structure can only be insured by one NFIP building coverage policy.
    • The residential structure data from the USACE National Structure Inventory (NSI) 2022 dataset may be incomplete in some communities. As such, there are cases where a block group has zero residential structures but still has positive numbers of NFIP residential contracts. The takeup rate is marked “not applicable” where a block group has no residential structures, and the rate in or outside the SFHA is marked “not applicable” where there are no residential structures in or outside the SFHA. Block groups with no residential structures are shown in light gray on the map.
    • NFIP takeup rates are hidden for block groups with fewer than 5 residential structures, as estimates can be noisy. These block groups appear on the map with a gray hatch pattern, and their contract counts are still shown.
