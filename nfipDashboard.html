    Methodology

    How the takeup rates on this dashboard are built, what each number means, and where the limits are.

    What the dashboard shows

    For every census block group in the 50 states and the District of Columbia, the dashboard shows the NFIP residential takeup rate: the share of residential structures with an active National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) contract. It reports the rate for all residential structures, for those inside the Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA), and for those outside it. The SFHA is the FEMA-mapped floodplain with a 1% chance of flooding in any given year.

    Data sources

    • NFIP residential contract-in-force: OpenFEMA dataset FIMA NFIP Redacted Policies, v3. Contracts are counted as active as of the date in the asOfDate field (currently —).
    • Residential structures: USACE National Structure Inventory (NSI): 2022 dataset. Residential only.
    • Flood zones: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL). Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs) are labeled as Zone A, Zone AO, Zone AH, Zones A1-A30, Zone AE, Zone A99, Zone AR, Zone AR/AE, Zone AR/AO, Zone AR/A1-A30, Zone AR/A, Zone V, Zone VE, and Zones V1-V30.
    • Boundaries: Census block groups; 2020 cartographic boundary files from the U.S. Census Bureau.

    How the rates are calculated

    NFIP residential takeup rate = active NFIP residential contracts ÷ residential structures
    Takeup rate in SFHA = active contracts in the SFHA ÷ residential structures in the SFHA
    Takeup rate outside SFHA = active contracts outside the SFHA ÷ residential structures outside the SFHA

    County, state, and national rates on the dashboard are calculated by summing counts across block groups and then dividing, not by averaging block group rates. This weights each area by its number of residential structures.

    Small numbers

    Rates are hidden for block groups with fewer than 5 residential structures, because one contract more or less would swing the rate dramatically. These block groups appear on the map with a gray hatch pattern, and their contract counts are still shown. The takeup rate in the SFHA is marked “not applicable” where a block group has no residential structures in the SFHA.

    Important notes:

    • Data for residential properties only.
    • Private flood insurance is not included in these estimates.
    • A contract may cover several units, and a structure can carry more than one contract, so rates can occasionally exceed 100%.
    • The SFHA doesn’t capture all flood risk. Many damaging floods happen outside it, and some areas have not been mapped by FEMA.
    • The data is a snapshot of contracts in force on one date. Takeup changes as contracts lapse and renew.
